### Thesis

The body is the oldest archive. Posture, gesture, breath — all carry memory that language cannot hold. Documentary practice that ignores this is incomplete.

We film faces and we miss the hands. We record speech and we miss the held breath before it. We edit for meaning and we cut the silences where meaning lives.

### On Gesture

Gesture is not illustration of speech. It is a parallel channel — sometimes confirming, sometimes contradicting, sometimes operating on a frequency that has no verbal equivalent.

The filmmaker's task is to be present to both registers: what is said, and what the body does while saying it.

### On Breath

Breath marks time. It marks effort, fear, grief, relief. It is one of the most intimate things a camera can record — and one of the first things cut in post.

*Fibres* will not cut breath. The sound of eating, of breathing, of the body at work: these are the score.

### The Archive as Somatic

Institutional archives store documents. The somatic archive stores sensation: what it felt like to stand in a particular kitchen, to hear a particular voice. This cannot be retrieved — only approximated, evoked.

Documentary as approximation: honest about what it cannot recover, committed to the attempt.
