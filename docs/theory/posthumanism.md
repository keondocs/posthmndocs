### Karen Barad — Intra-action

The concept that keeps returning: *intra-action*.

Not interaction — which would presume two pre-existing entities who then come into contact. Intra-action means the entities themselves emerge through the encounter. There is no filmmaker and subject who then meet. The meeting makes both.

For documentary this is not a metaphor. It is a description of what actually happens in the room. The moment I enter with a camera, I am not recording a reality that pre-exists me. I am participating in the production of a new one. The subject performs differently because I am there. I think differently because they are in front of me. We are co-constituting each other in real time.

Barad calls this a *phenomenon* — not the object observed, not the observer, but the inseparability of both. The documentary is not a representation of a phenomenon. It *is* one.

The apparatus matters too. Not just the camera but everything: the lighting, the edit, the funding structure, the room, the crew, the relationship that preceded the shoot. All of it participates in what gets made real. None of it is neutral infrastructure. The apparatus is agential — it cuts, it enacts, it produces boundaries between what is visible and what is not.

This is where documentary ethics begins: not in the intention of the filmmaker, but in the configuration of the apparatus itself.

### Donna Haraway — Situated Knowledge

No view from nowhere.

Every knowledge claim is made from a body, a location, a history. The god-trick — the pretence of seeing everything from no particular place — is not objectivity. It is a power move that refuses to account for itself.

Situated knowledge does not mean relativism. It means accountability. It means saying: *this is where I am standing, this is what I can and cannot see from here, and I take responsibility for that position.*

For documentary: the filmmaker is always somewhere. The question is whether they admit it. The camera angle, the choice of location, the duration of the take, the decision to cut — all of these are claims made from a specific position. Making that position visible is not weakness. It is rigour.

Haraway also gave us the figure of the cyborg — the being that refuses clean boundaries between organism and machine, nature and culture, self and other. The filmmaker with a camera is already a cyborg. The question is whether the work knows this about itself.

### Rosi Braidotti — Nomadic Ethics and Vital Materialism

The body is not a container for a self. It is a *time machine* — enfleshed memory, relational matter, capable of lasting through discontinuous variations of intensity.

The people I film are not sources of information. They are ongoing processes of becoming. To reduce them to what they say in an interview — to extract their content and discard the rest — is to misunderstand what a body is.

Braidotti's nomadic ethics asks the filmmaker to *deterritorialise* — to release the grip of their own artistic vision, not in order to erase themselves, but to make space. The director's vision is the majority, the *sameness*. The subject's identity is the minority, the *otherness*. A nomadic ethics refuses the opposition between them. It asks: how can the encounter transform both?

This is not self-erasure. It is a different kind of rigour. It requires, as Braidotti writes, an enormous effort of self-reflection — not on the Eurocentric self of the creative economy, but on the self that is part of a collective, a community, the otherness.

Desire, in this framework, is not lack. It is *plenitude* — giving what you did not even suspect you had in you. The filmmaker's desire to make the work is not a need to possess the subject's story. It is an overflowing that seeks connection, transformation, encounter.

What is affirmed is desire, freedom, and becoming.

### Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari — Rhizome and Becoming

The rhizome against the tree.

Western knowledge tends to organise itself arborescently — root, trunk, branch, leaf. One origin, one direction, one hierarchy. The canon. The archive as monument.

The rhizome is different: horizontal spread, multiple entry points, no fixed origin, no correct reading order. It grows sideways. It connects across. It has no centre.

This is how I want this archive to grow — not as a canon but as a field. Not a reading list but a network of relations between ideas, images, people, practices. You should be able to enter it anywhere and find your way through.

Deleuze and Guattari also give us *becoming* over *being*. The filmmaker is not an author who stands outside the work and gives it meaning. The filmmaker is one node in a network of forces, bodies, times — momentarily stabilised into something called a film, which then dissolves again into the network. Authorship as a temporary crystallisation, not a fixed identity.

*Becoming-imperceptible* as a horizon: not disappearing, but becoming so thoroughly part of the material that the distinction between maker and made no longer holds.

### Trinh T. Minh-ha — Speaking Nearby

To speak *about* someone is to master them.

It claims full knowledge. It positions the speaker above the subject, translating their experience into terms the audience can consume. It closes off what it touches.

To speak *nearby* is to refuse that mastery. It means staying close to the subject without claiming to contain them. It means making a film that acknowledges the gap between the person and any representation of them — and treats that gap as something to be respected, not filled.

Opacity as an ethical stance: the subject does not owe you their transparency. Legibility is not the goal. A person's full complexity may be precisely what cannot and should not be rendered visible — and the documentary that honours this will find other ways to be true.

This is also a formal question. Long takes, silence, the refusal of explanatory voiceover, the decision not to subtitle — these are not stylistic choices. They are positions on what a person is owed.

---



These are not frameworks to apply to filmmaking from the outside. They are questions that arise *from* the practice itself — from spending time with people, with material, with the limits of representation.
