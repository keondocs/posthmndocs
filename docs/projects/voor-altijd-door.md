### Where it started

Five years ago I made my first short documentary. I followed a friend, nineteen years old, to his audition for the music academy. He got in. At the end of the film, he proclaimed: "Ik ga voor altijd door." I will always keep going.

I made the same promise to myself, quietly, behind the lens.

Now we are six years down the road. He is twenty-five, works full-time during the day, and makes music until late at night. I am thirty-two, making documentaries, also often until late at night. He still messages me: "Yo bro! When are we going to shoot a music video?" We have not made a music video together in three years.

I keep asking myself: when do we arrive? And what does it cost us to keep going in the meantime?

### What it asks

Voor Altijd Door follows my friend and me as we navigate the pressure we place on ourselves as creatives. The performance of ambition. The gap between who we are online and who we are at two in the morning when nothing is working. The way we have both developed multiple personas to survive in an attention economy that rewards surface and punishes rest.

We are both one-man operations. My friend sings, drums, produces, mixes. I direct, film, edit, grade, design sound. That autonomy is also a kind of loneliness. And underneath it is a question neither of us has fully answered: what does it mean to keep going without losing yourself in the process?

The film does not treat this as a problem to be solved. It treats it as a condition to be examined, slowly, from the inside.

### What makes it posthumanist

The film is interested in identity not as something fixed but as something performed, assembled, revised. How we build personas, how those personas take on a life of their own, and what remains when the camera stops.

Voor Altijd Door does not claim that authenticity is simply there waiting to be found. It asks what authenticity even means when the self is always already mediated, always already in relation to an attention economy that rewards surface and punishes rest.

Our creative community is not a backdrop in this film. It is part of the argument. Because neither of us got here alone, even when it felt that way.

### Form

Feature documentary, approximately 45 minutes. The structural spine is my friend's album: each track forms a filmic chapter. Around that spine run a few threads that are currently being developed.

The film draws from documentary and music film equally. It is as interested in the space between songs as in the songs themselves.
